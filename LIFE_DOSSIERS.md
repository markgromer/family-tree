# Life Dossiers

This file turns the family tree into researched lives.

The standard is not merely **who begat whom**. For each confirmed ancestor or useful collateral relative, research:

- occupations and employers
- businesses owned
- military service, rank, units, deployments, medals
- education
- homes, farms, land, mortgages, property values
- census household and class/economic clues
- probate / estate
- churches, clubs, unions, civic groups
- newspapers and advertisements
- lawsuits, arrests, criminal cases, bankruptcy — only with strong identity matching
- migration and why the person moved
- hobbies and personality clues
- photographs, yearbooks, signatures, documents
- major historical events that materially touched the person's life

Do not label someone "rich", "poor", "criminal", etc. from weak evidence. Record the underlying facts, then contextualize them against the place and period.

## Economic / class research protocol

For each ancestor, collect the evidence before summarizing class or wealth:

1. occupation over time
2. owned vs rented housing/farm
3. property value and personal estate in census records
4. acreage and land deeds
5. employees / servants / hired hands
6. probate estate
7. taxes
8. business ownership
9. bankruptcy / liens / foreclosure
10. comparison with local households in the same period

Only after that should the narrative use language such as working class, prosperous farmer, substantial landowner, financially distressed, etc.

## Legal / criminal research protocol

Court or criminal findings require unusually strong identity matching:
- full name
- age/date
- place
- spouse/relative corroboration where possible
- source type
- disposition/outcome

Do not turn an arrest into a conviction, or a same-name newspaper item into a family fact.
